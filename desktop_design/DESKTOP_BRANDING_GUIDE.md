# Desktop Branding Guide

## Brand Voice and Tone
VaultPass speaks to its users with a voice that is:
- **Authoritative & Secure:** We deal with critical personal data. The language should inspire absolute confidence. Avoid overly playful or colloquial language.
- **Transparent:** Never obscure what the app is doing. If an action permanently deletes data, say so clearly. If data is encrypted, explain how.
- **Privacy-First:** Reiterate the offline-first nature of the app where appropriate. Remind users that *we* cannot reset their passwords.

## Name Usage
- **Correct:** VaultPass (One word, CamelCase).
- **Incorrect:** Vault Pass, Vaultpass, vaultpass.

## Logo Usage
- **Primary Logo:** A stylized, minimalist Vault/Shield icon. 
- **Clear Space:** Always maintain a padding around the logo equal to at least 25% of the logo's width to prevent crowding.
- **Iconography:** Keep the logo sharp and legible at small sizes (e.g., `16x16` for the system tray). Ensure there is a high-contrast monochromatic version for dark mode system trays (like Windows 11).

## Typography
- VaultPass utilizes standard, clean sans-serif fonts. 
- **Brand Font:** `Inter` is preferred for marketing materials and large UI headings to maintain a sleek, modern tech aesthetic.

## Imagery
- Do not use cartoonish illustrations or 3D renders of padlocks.
- Rely on high-quality typography, subtle gradients, and data visualization (like the Security Center graphs) to provide visual interest.
- The UI *is* the brand. A clean, uncluttered, and fast interface reinforces the brand promise of efficiency and security.
